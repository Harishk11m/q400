Aircraft Maintenance Procedures

Flow (Pack) Control and Shutoff Valve (21-20-3)

The maintenance procedure involves manually securing the valve in the open position. This is done by accessing the valve in the Aft Equipment Bay, removing a locking pin, turning a crank to the open position, and then re-installing the pin to lock it. After securing the valve, a series of checks must be performed with the engines running to verify the operability of the Nacelle Shutoff Valves and the ECU's digital (AUTO) channels. Finally, the valve must be placarded in the flight compartment, and a log entry must be made.

SIDE WINDOW DEMIST Vent Controls (21-20-5-2)

The maintenance procedure requires the affected vent to be secured in the open position. This is achieved by removing the cockpit sidewall trim panel, accessing the Side Window Demist Outlet Flow Control Valve, and moving its lever to the fully open position. The panel is then re-installed, the system is placarded as inoperative, and an entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Low Level Vent Controls (21-20-6B)

To render the vent inoperative, it must be confirmed and moved to the closed position. This requires removing an access panel on the nose fuselage to get to the Low Level Vent Flow Control Valve. The lever is then moved to the fully closed position, the panel is re-installed, and the system is placarded as inoperative on the AIR CONDITIONING Panel. An appropriate entry is then made in the aircraft journey log.

Aft Baggage Compartment Vent Valves (21-20-8-1A)

The maintenance procedure is to deactivate the inlet and outlet vent valves, which must be in the closed position. This is done by pulling and clipping the appropriate circuit breakers on the R Main Circuit Breaker panel. The procedure also requires verifying that the "CLOSED" advisory lights for the vent valves are illuminated on the Fire Protection Panel. Finally, a placard is placed on the panel, and a log entry is made.

Cabin Pressure Control System

AUTO and MAN Modes (21-30-5-1)

The maintenance procedure involves verifying the operation of the FWD OUTFLOW valve, the DUMP function, and the AFT OUTFLOW valve. This is an extensive process that includes applying DC power, starting an engine or APU, and performing a series of steps to pressurize and depressurize the cabin using manual controls to check valve operation and pressure indicators. The procedure culminates with placarding the system as inoperative and making a log entry.

AUTO Mode (21-30-5-2)

The maintenance procedure focuses on verifying the manual control, DUMP function, and FWD Safety (OUTFLOW) Valve control on the ground. This involves powering the aircraft, starting an engine or APU, and performing a sequence of pressurization and depressurization steps using the manual controls to check the functionality of the pressure and altitude indicators. The system is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

MAN Mode (21-30-5-3)

The maintenance procedure involves verifying the automatic control, DUMP function, and FWD Safety (OUTFLOW) valve DUMP function. This includes a sequence of steps with the engine or APU running to check the cabin rate, altitude, and differential pressure indications while switching between different control modes. The system is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

Aft Outflow Valve (21-30-6-1)

The procedure requires verifying that the aft outflow valve is open and that the aircraft does not allow pressurization. This is done by attempting to pressurize the cabin using the manual differential switch while monitoring the cabin rate, differential, and altitude indications. The AUTO/MAN/DUMP switch is then set to DUMP, and the engines are shut down. The system is placarded with "AUTO/MAN MODES INOP - SWITCH MUST REMAIN IN DUMP" and a log entry is made.

Aft Safety Valve (21-30-6-2)

The maintenance procedure is identical to the one for the Aft Outflow Valve. It involves a series of steps to verify that the aircraft does not pressurize and that the cabin altitude does not change. The system is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

Turbine Shut-Off Valves (21-50-2)

The maintenance procedure involves manually securing the valve in the closed position. This is done by removing a locking pin, turning a crank on the valve to the closed position, and then re-installing the pin to keep it locked. The associated PACK switch on the AIR CONDITIONING panel is then placarded as "INOP," and a log entry is made.

Pack Bypass Valves (21-50-3)

The maintenance procedure requires manually operating and locking the valve in the closed position. This is accomplished by removing a locking screw, turning the valve with a wrench, and then re-installing the locking screw in a new position to secure the valve. The associated PACKS switch on the AIR CONDITIONING panel is placarded as "INOP," and a log entry is made.

CABIN TEMP CONTROL

AUTO and MAN Modes (21-60-1-1)

The maintenance procedure involves verifying that the Flight Compartment Temperature Control is operative. This is done by starting an engine or APU, setting the AIR CONDITIONING controls to manual, and then checking the temperature trends on the gauge as the control knob is rotated between "full WARM" and "full COOL". The system is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

AUTO Mode (21-60-1-2)

The procedure requires verifying that the Manual (MAN) control is operative. This is done by starting an engine or APU, setting the controls to manual, and then observing the temperature trends on the gauge as the cabin temperature control knob is rotated between "full WARM" and "full COOL". The system is then placarded as inoperative, and a log entry is made.

MAN Mode (21-60-1-3)

The procedure requires verifying that the Automatic (AUTO) control is operative. This is done by starting an engine or APU, setting the controls to automatic, and then observing the temperature trends on the gauge as the cabin temperature control knob is rotated between "full WARM" and "full COOL". The system is then placarded as inoperative, and a log entry is made.

FLT COMP TEMP

AUTO and MAN Modes (21-60-3-1)

The procedure involves verifying that the CABIN TEMP CONTROL (AUTO & MANUAL) is operative. This is done by starting an engine or APU, setting the controls to manual, and then observing the temperature trends on the gauge as the cabin temperature control knob is rotated between "full WARM" and "full COOL" for both manual and automatic modes. The system is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

AUTO Mode (21-60-3-2)

The procedure requires verifying that the Manual (MAN) control is operative. This is done by starting an engine or APU, setting the controls to manual, and then observing the temperature trends on the gauge as the flight compartment temperature control knob is rotated between "full WARM" and "full COOL". The system is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

MAN Mode (21-60-3-3)

The procedure requires verifying that the Automatic (AUTO) control is operative. This is done by starting an engine or APU, setting the controls to automatic, and then observing the temperature trends on the gauge as the flight compartment temperature control knob is rotated between "full WARM" and "full COOL". The system is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

Yaw Damper System (22-10-5)

If the yaw damper is inoperative, the autopilot will also be unavailable, and CAT II operations are not permitted. The maintenance procedure involves pulling and clipping three specific circuit breakers on the avionics circuit breaker panel: YD AU (E3), APAU ROLL (E2), and APAU PITCH (E1). If the yaw damper actuator has failed, a rudder travel check must be performed to confirm full rudder movement. If the actuator is stuck in an extended or retracted position, it must be replaced before dispatch. Finally, the "AP/YD INOP" placard must be placed on the flight guidance control panel, and a log entry must be made.

A/P DIS Switches (22-10-6-1)

If the autopilot disengage switches are inoperative, the autopilot itself is considered inoperative and cannot be used. The maintenance procedure requires placing a placard on the control wheel switch(es). If both switches are inoperative, the Flight Guidance Controller (Autopilot Switch) on the glareshield panel must also be placarded. An appropriate entry is then made in the aircraft journey log.

Active Noise and Vibration Suppression System (23-30-4)

The system may be inoperative, but it is recommended to keep the controller installed and powered on until a replacement is available. Instead of turning the system off, the PAUSE button should be pressed. If a hammering noise is heard from the Active Tuned Vibration Attenuators (ATVAs), the system should be selected to ANVS OFF on the control panel, or the associated circuit breakers on the right DC circuit breaker panel should be opened and clipped. Prolonged periods with the system off or with open circuit breakers can damage the ATVAs. When the system is inoperative, the Propeller Balancing Monitoring System (PBMS) will also be unavailable. Finally, the Forward Flight Attendant's Panel must be placarded with "ANVS INOP," and an entry must be made in the aircraft journey log.

Boom Microphone Push-To-Talk (PTT) Switches (23-50-1)

This item has different procedures depending on the failure condition.

PTT switch that fails to transmit

No maintenance check is required. You simply need to placard the switch as "INOP" and make an entry in the aircraft journey log.

PTT switch that causes continuous transmission

  • Pilot and Co-pilot Control Column PTT Switch: This procedure requires opening several specific circuit breakers, pulling back the control column, and physically disconnecting the wires to the PTT switch. The disconnected wires are then taped and stowed. After closing the circuit breakers, a check is performed to confirm that no voice communication is possible over the opposite cockpit speaker. The column is then placarded "PTT INOP," and a log entry is made.
  • PTT Switch on Nose Wheel Steering Tiller: This involves opening three specific circuit breakers, removing a side panel to access a connector, and removing a specific wire from a pin. The wire is then taped and stowed, the connector is reconnected, and the circuit breakers are closed. A test is performed to confirm no voice communication is possible, followed by a hydraulic system check to confirm nosewheel travel. The system is placarded "PTT INOP," and a log entry is made.
  • XMIT Switch on the Co-pilot Side Console: This procedure involves opening three specific circuit breakers, removing a side panel to access a connector, and removing a specific wire. The wire is taped and stowed, the connector is reconnected, and the side panel and circuit breakers are re-installed. A check is then performed to ensure no voice communication is possible, the switch is placarded "XMIT INOP," and a log entry is made.

Transformer Rectifier Units (TRUs) (24-30-1)

If a Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU) is inoperative, it must be deactivated. This is done by opening and clipping the failed TRU circuit breaker on the 115 VAC circuit breaker panel. If the TRU's cooling fan is the cause of the failure or needs to be deactivated, a maintenance technician must disconnect the impacted TRU from the DC Contactor Box and cover the electrical connections with a non-conductive material. After deactivation, a series of checks are performed with the engines running to verify the operation of the electrical system, including bus voltages and load indications. The inoperative TRU is placarded on the CAUTION LIGHT panel, and an entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

DC Starter/Generation Systems (24-30-6)

If one DC Generator is inoperative in the generation mode, the cause of the malfunction must be determined, and a visual inspection must be performed for signs of overheat, leakage, or wire damage. The maintenance procedure involves verifying that the start function of the affected engine operates normally. After starting the engine, the failed DC Generator switch is selected to the OFF position on the DC CONTROL Panel. Then, a check is performed to ensure that both Transformer Rectifier Units (TRUs) are verified operative by checking for caution lights and electrical load indications on the ESID. The inoperative DC Starter/Generator is placarded at the DC CONTROL panel, and an entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Forward Observer Seat (25-10-1)

The maintenance procedure for this item is to placard the seat as inoperative and make an entry in the aircraft journey log. The seat must be removed, stowed, or secured in a retracted position.

Pilot's Seat Adjustments (25-10-2)

Vertical Mode Adjustment (25-10-2-1)

To address an inoperative vertical adjustment, the pilot's seat can be manually positioned to the desired height. This is done by unseating the spring-loaded metering pins to move the seat, then allowing them to engage and lock in the desired position. The seat is then placarded as "INOPERATIVE" and a log entry is made.

Fore/Aft Mode (25-10-2-2)

For an inoperative fore/aft adjustment, the seat must be secured in a position that meets the pilot's requirements. The procedure involves manually unseating the metering pins to adjust the seat's position and then allowing them to lock into place. The inoperative seat is then placarded, and an entry is made in the journey log.

Crew Armrests (25-10-2-3)

The maintenance procedure requires the inoperative armrest(s) to be physically removed from the seat. After removal, a log entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Recline Mechanism (25-10-2-6)

If the recline mechanism is inoperative, the seat back must be secured in a position acceptable to the crew member. This is done by manually unseating the recline adjustment metering pins to adjust the backrest angle, and then allowing them to lock in place. The inoperative seat is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

Pilot's Seat Heaters (25-10-3)

Maintenance for an inoperative heater (25-10-3-1)

The inoperative heater must be deactivated. The procedure is to open and clip the "FLT CREW SEAT HTRS" circuit breaker, disconnect the appropriate electrical connector for the pilot's or co-pilot's seat, and then cap and stow the connector. The circuit breaker is then reset. The affected seat is placarded "SEAT HEATER INOP," and a log entry is made.

Maintenance for one inoperative heater (25-10-3-2)

The procedure requires opening and clipping the "FLT CREW SEAT HTRS" circuit breaker on the Right DC Circuit Breaker Panel. The affected seat is then placarded "SEAT HEATER INOP" and a log entry is made.

Overhead, Cabin and Galley Storage Bins/Compartments/Closets (25-20-2)

Inoperative but Closed (25-20-2A)

The maintenance procedure requires the storage bin, compartment, or closet to be placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT USE". All items must be removed except for those permanently affixed, and the bin/door must be secured in the closed position. An entry is then made in the aircraft journey log.

Inoperative with door removed (25-20-2B)

The maintenance procedure is to remove the door or lid of the inoperative compartment. The compartment must be emptied, and if any emergency equipment was on the removed door, it must be relocated to the nearest feasible location and secured for easy access. The original and new locations of the equipment are placarded, and the compartment itself is placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT USE". A log entry is also required.

Inoperative Key Locks (25-20-2-2)

If a storage compartment key lock is inoperative in the unlocked position, the compartment must be secured by other means. The maintenance must ensure that the security of the compartment is not significantly degraded by the alternate means. If it is, any equipment that requires to be locked must be relocated or removed. An entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Flight Attendant Seat (25-20-4)

Required Seats (25-20-4-1)

An inoperative flight attendant seat must be placarded "INOPERATIVE". If it is a folding seat, it must be secured in the retracted (up) position. If it cannot be secured, the seat pan must be removed. The passenger seat assigned for the displaced flight attendant must be placarded "FOR FLIGHT ATTENDANT USE ONLY". A log entry is also required.

Excess Equipment (25-20-4-2)

An inoperative flight attendant seat that is not required can be placarded and secured in the retracted position or removed. The maintenance procedure specifies placarding the seat as "INOPERATIVE" and securing folding seats in the retracted position. If the seat pan can't be secured, it must be removed. A log entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Passenger Seats (25-20-5)

Inoperative seat (25-20-5)

If a passenger seat is inoperative, it must be blocked and placarded "DO NOT OCCUPY". This may also apply to adjacent or aft seats if they restrict access to the aisle. An entry must be made in the aircraft journey log.

Inoperative Recline Mechanism (25-20-5-1)

The maintenance procedure requires securing the seat back in the full upright position. The process for doing this varies depending on the seat model. For Model 925/935, it involves removing the bottom cushion, rotating a clear plastic barrel to move a brass tab, and then reinstalling a snap ring to lock the mechanism. For Slimplus models, it requires installing additional spacers into the recline mechanism.

Armrest with Recline Mechanism (25-20-5-2)

If an armrest is inoperative, damaged, or missing, the seat back must be secured in the full upright position. If the armrest restricts access to the aisle, the seat must be placarded "DO NOT OCCUPY" and blocked from use. If the armrest is not in a horizontal position and restricts access, it must be removed, and the seat back secured upright. An entry is made in the log.

Armrest without Recline Mechanism (25-20-5-3)

If the armrest is inoperative, damaged, or missing and restricts access to the aircraft aisle, the seat must be placarded "DO NOT OCCUPY" and blocked. If the armrest is not in a horizontal position and restricts aisle access, it must be removed. A log entry is also required.

Tray table in the unstowed position (25-20-5-4)

If a tray table is inoperative in the unstowed position, the affected seats must be blocked and placarded "DO NOT OCCUPY". For seat back tray tables, this includes the seat immediately aft of the inoperative one. If the tray table blocks aisle access, all affected seats must be blocked and placarded. An entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Airstair Door Ditching Dam (25-60-6)

The maintenance procedure for an inoperative ditching dam requires it to be secured in the stowed position. This is done by taping the upper edge and half of the left and right edges to the surrounding interior. The ditching dam handle is also taped over. Placards indicating "DITCHING DAM INOPERATIVE" must be posted on the pilot's glare shield panel and the ditching dam itself. A log entry is also made.

Flight Deck Sun Visors (25-60-7-2)

If a sun visor is inoperative, it must be removed. The procedure for removing the sun visor assembly is described, including supporting the assembly and removing screws and washers. An entry is then made in the aircraft journey log.

Flight Attendant Flashlight Holders (25-60-4-2)

If the flashlight holder is inoperative, a flashlight must be secured near the holder using masking tape to prevent movement. The flashlight must not be removed from its original compartment. The normal stowage location must be placarded with a direction to the alternate stowage location. A log entry is also made.

Flight Deck Flashlight Holders (25-60-8-2)

If a flashlight holder is inoperative or missing, an alternate stowage location must be determined in the flight compartment where the flashlight's movement will be constrained during accelerations and be accessible to the crew. A placard must be placed at the normal stowage location with a direction to the alternate location. An entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Galley Waste Compartment (25-30-1)

The inoperative door or flap must be taped to secure it in a closed position. The container inside must be emptied, and access to it must be secured to prevent waste from being put in. The door or flap is then placarded as inoperative, and an entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Aft Baggage Divider Nets (25-50-1)

The maintenance procedure for an inoperative or missing aft baggage divider net is to completely remove the inoperative net and its associated mounting hardware. Ground crew must be notified of the missing net. The technician must ensure no loose items remain in the compartment that could interfere with baggage loading or door closure. A log entry is then made.

Aft Baggage Door Threshold Net (25-50-2)

Damaged Net (25-50-2-1)

The maintenance procedure for a damaged net involves inspecting the damage to ensure it is within specified limits, such as no more than six detached stitching locations in total and no more than two per nylon strap. If a clip is unattached, the loose strap end should be secured with a tie wrap. The exterior of the aft baggage compartment door must be placarded with a warning about the damaged or missing net. A log entry is also required.

Missing Net (25-50-2-2)

The maintenance procedure is to completely remove the inoperative net assembly from the threshold protector. It must be ensured that no loose items remain in the compartment that could interfere with baggage loading or door closure. A placard indicating the missing net is placed on the exterior of the aft baggage compartment door, and a log entry is made.

Lavatory Smoke Detection System

Failure to Test (26-10-1A)

If the smoke detector fails to test, the lavatory must not be used by passengers, its waste receptacle must be empty, and the lavatory door must be locked closed and placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT ENTER". The lavatory may only be used by crew members, and in-flight service waste bags cannot be stored inside it. Maintenance procedures involve placarding the system in the flight compartment, emptying the waste receptacle, and locking the lavatory door.

Intermittent or Continuous Failure (26-10-1B)

If the smoke detector shows an intermittent or continuous failure, it must be deactivated. The maintenance procedure involves pulling the circuit breaker for the lavatory smoke detector, gaining access to the detector, and disconnecting its electrical connector. After stowing the connector, the circuit breaker is reset. Similar to the previous item, the lavatory is placarded, the waste receptacle is emptied, and the door is locked.

Non-Passenger Operations (26-10-1-1B)

For non-passenger operations, if the smoke detector fails intermittently or continuously, it must be deactivated. This is done by pulling the circuit breaker "SMK DET LAV/CG01," gaining access to the smoke detector, and disconnecting its electrical connector. The circuit breaker is then reset. The system is placarded in the flight compartment, and an entry is made in the aircraft journey log.

Aft Baggage Compartment Smoke Detectors

One Detector Inoperative (26-10-2-1)

If one smoke detector is inoperative, it must be deactivated. This involves pulling the appropriate circuit breaker, disconnecting the electrical connector from the smoke detector, and then resetting the breaker. In addition, all baggage compartment vent valves must be considered inoperative and deactivated in the closed position by pulling and clipping their circuit breakers. The placards are then updated to reflect that both the smoke detector and vent valves are inoperative.

One or More Detectors Inoperative (26-10-2-2)

If one or more smoke detectors are inoperative, they must be deactivated. The maintenance procedure is to pull the relevant circuit breaker(s) for the smoke detector(s), disconnect the electrical connector(s) at the units, and then reset the breaker(s). The baggage compartment must be empty, or contain only approved ballast. A placard is placed on the Fire Protection Panel indicating the inoperative smoke detectors, and a log entry is made.

Forward Baggage Compartment Smoke Detector (26-10-3)

If the forward baggage compartment smoke detector is inoperative, it must be deactivated. This is done by pulling the circuit breaker for "BAG/CGO2," disconnecting the electrical connector from the unit, and then resetting the breaker. The baggage compartment must be empty or contain only approved ballast. A log entry is made.

APU Fire Detection System (26-10-4)

The APU fire detection system must be deactivated. The procedure requires turning off all aircraft electrical power, gaining access to the APU compartment's Advanced Pneumatic Detector (APD), and disconnecting the electrical connector from it. Jumper pins are used to connect pins on the disconnected connector, which is then taped and stowed. The connector on the APD is capped. After applying DC power and ensuring the "CHECK FIRE DET" warning light is out, the APU fuel shut-off valve's closed position must be verified. This can be done by a visual check of the position indicator arm or by a functional test of the APU start sequence. The APU Control and APU Fire Protection Panels are placarded, and a log entry is made.

APU Fire Extinguishing System (26-20-1)

The APU fire extinguishing system is deactivated. This involves turning off all electrical power, gaining access to the fire extinguishing bottle, and disconnecting the electrical connector from the pressure switch. Jumper pins are used to connect two pins on the disconnected connector, which is then taped and stowed. The connector on the fire bottle is capped. After restoring power and confirming the "CHECK FIRE DET" warning light is extinguished, the APU fuel shut-off valve's closed position is verified. The APU control and fire protection panels are placarded, and a log entry is made.

Baggage Compartment Fire Extinguishers

Low Rate Discharge (LRD) Bottle (26-20-4-1)

The Low Rate Discharge (LRD) bottle must be deactivated. The procedure requires turning off all electrical power, accessing the bottle, and disconnecting its electrical connector. The disconnected connector is jumpered, taped, and stowed, and the connector on the fire bottle is capped. The forward and aft baggage compartments must be empty or contain only approved ballast and placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT USE". The Fire Protection Panel is placarded, and a log entry is made.

High Rate Discharge (HRD) Bottle (26-20-4-2)

The High Rate Discharge (HRD) bottle must be deactivated. The procedure involves turning off all electrical power, accessing the bottle, and disconnecting its electrical connector. The disconnected connector is jumpered, taped, and stowed, and the connector on the fire bottle is capped. The associated baggage compartment must be empty or contain only approved ballast and placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT USE". The Fire Protection Panel is placarded, and a log entry is made.

AIL TRIM Indicator (27-10-1)

To address an inoperative aileron trim indicator, the maintenance procedure involves performing a visual check to confirm the aileron has full, free, and correct movement and is in a neutral position. This check is done by having an observer confirm aileron deflection as the aileron trim switch is moved. The inoperative indicator is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

Rudder Pedal Adjustment (27-20-2)

When a rudder pedal adjustment is inoperative, the pedals must be in a position that meets the pilot's requirements and the mechanism must be secured from moving. The procedure involves manually checking that the rudder pedals and adjustment shaft do not move. A visual inspection of the adjustment lever and rudder input mechanism is required by opening an access panel. The pedals are then checked to ensure they move the rudder correctly without any unwanted movement in the adjustment mechanism. Finally, the inoperative rudder pedal adjustment is placarded, and a log entry is made.

Stick Shaker (27-30-2)

If a stick shaker is inoperative, it must be deactivated. The maintenance procedure is to tape and stow the electrical connector of the affected stick shaker. The unaffected stick shaker must be tested before each flight. The glareshield must be placarded with "L or R STICK SHAKER INOP," and a log entry must be made.

#1 STALL SYSTEM FAIL, #2 STALL SYSTEM FAIL, Caution Light(s) (27-30-3)

The maintenance procedure for this item varies depending on the annunciated failure. In all cases, the procedure involves pulling and then resetting specific circuit breakers (WOW2 + CONT) to confirm caution lights extinguish and return. Before each flight, the Centralized Diagnostic System must be interrogated to confirm the specific reason for the lights and ensure the failure is not related to the propeller de-ice system. The glareshield is then placarded with the failed function, and a log entry is made.

Stall Warning System (27-30-4)

If one stall warning system is inoperative, the affected system and the stick pusher must be deactivated. The maintenance procedure involves pulling and clipping the STICK PUSHER circuit breakers on the avionics panel. If the #1 system is affected, the circuit breaker for SPM 1 should be pulled. However, swapping SPMs is recommended to keep SPM 1 operational, as pulling its breaker also disables the GPWS. The unaffected system must be tested before each flight. The glareshield is placarded with "# STALL SYST / PUSHER INOP" and "GPWS INOP" if applicable, and a log entry is made.

Stick Pusher System (27-30-5)

If the stick pusher system is inoperative, it must be deactivated. The maintenance procedure involves pulling and clipping the STICK PUSHER circuit breakers on the avionics circuit breaker panel. The stick shakers for both the pilot and co-pilot must be tested to ensure they operate correctly and their respective caution lights do not illuminate. A placard is placed on the glareshield with "# STALL SYST/PUSHER INOP," and a log entry is made.

Roll Spoiler Caution Lights (27-60-1)

If the ROLL SPLR INBD or OUTBD GND caution light is inoperative, the associated roll spoiler ground mode must be deactivated. The maintenance procedure involves disconnecting, capping, and securing the electrical connector to the lift dump valve associated with the applicable spoiler. The hydraulic systems are then pressurized, and a visual check is performed to confirm that all spoilers are in the down position and have full, free movement. A placard is placed on the Caution/Warning Panel, and a log entry is made.

Roll Spoilers (Ground Mode System) (27-60-4)

When the Roll Spoilers Ground Mode System is inoperative, the associated roll spoiler ground mode must be deactivated. The maintenance procedure is to disconnect, cap, and secure the electrical connector to the lift dump valve for the applicable spoiler pair. After pressurizing the hydraulic systems, a visual check confirms that all spoilers are in the down position. The system is placarded as inoperative, and a log entry is made.

Refueling Systems

  • REFUEL (Manual Pressure Refueling System): The system may be inoperative if the fuel transfer system is working. Maintenance involves using the Multi-Function Display (MFD) to transfer fuel between tanks to verify the system is operative.
  • PRESELECT REFUEL (Automatic Pressure Refueling System): This system may be inoperative if the fuel transfer system is working. Maintenance is the same as the manual system, requiring a fuel transfer between tanks to verify the system.
  • REFUEL AND PRESELECT REFUEL (Both Systems): Both systems may be inoperative if the fuel transfer system is working and gravity refueling procedures are established. Maintenance requires verifying fuel transfer between tanks.
  • MASTER VALVE CLOSED Light: This light may be inoperative if the Refuel/Defuel Shut-Off Valve is confirmed closed after refueling. Maintenance requires visually checking the valve's position by removing an access panel.
  • Refuel/Defuel/Transfer VALVE (OPEN/CLOSED) Advisory Lights: The lights may be inoperative if the fuel transfer system and flight compartment fuel quantity indications are working. Maintenance involves applying power and using the transfer switch to verify fuel is moving between tanks and that the fuel quantity indicators are working.
  • External Refuel/Defuel Panel FUEL QTY Indicators: These may be inoperative as long as fuel quantity is determined by other approved means. Maintenance requires leveling the aircraft and determining fuel quantity using the flight compartment indicators or magnetic dipsticks after refueling.
  • REFUEL SHUTOFF TANK 1 and REFUEL SHUTOFF TANK 2 Advisory Lights: These may be inoperative if the flight compartment fuel quantity indications are working. Maintenance involves verifying that the fuel quantity indicators in the flight compartment and on the external panel have stopped increasing after refueling.
  • APU FUEL VALVE OPEN Advisory Light: This light may be inoperative if the OPEN light is extinguished and the CLOSED light is illuminated when the APU is off. Maintenance requires applying DC power and verifying the status of both lights on the Fire Control Panel.
  • APU FUEL VALVE CLOSED Advisory Light: This light may be inoperative if the APU is not operated and the shut-off valve is verified closed. There are three methods for verification: pulling a circuit breaker and attempting to start the APU, visually inspecting the valve with an access panel open, or using a borescope.
  • Flight Compartment Fuel QTY Indications (on ESID): One of these may be inoperative if fuel quantity is determined by other means before each flight, fuel flow indications are operative and monitored, and the TANK FUEL LOW caution light is operative. Maintenance involves using HMU dipsticks or external indicators to verify fuel quantity after refueling and verifying the low-level sensor by transferring fuel until the TANK FUEL LOW light illuminates.

Hydraulic System Indicators

HYD FSOV OPEN Indication Light

One of the two HYD FSOV OPEN indication lights may be inoperative if its associated hydraulic firewall shutoff valve (HYD FSOV) is verified open and the corresponding CLOSED indication light is operative. The maintenance procedure involves an operational test of the associated HYD FSOV. This includes placing ground lock pins, visually checking the valve position, pulling the fire handle to confirm it closes, and pushing the handle back in to confirm it re-opens.

HYD PRESS 1 and HYD PRESS 2 Indications

One of the HYD PRESS 1 or 2 indications may be inoperative if the associated ENG HYD PUMP Caution Light and HYD QTY Indication are verified operative. Maintenance involves checking the hydraulic fluid quantity and then starting the engine to verify the caution light goes out.

HYD QTY 3 Indication

The HYD QTY 3 indication may be inoperative if the System #3 Aft Fuselage Hydraulic Quantity Indicator is operative and adequate fluid quantity is verified before each flight. The maintenance procedure requires checking the hydraulic fluid quantity at the remote hydraulic quantity indicator in the aft fuselage equipment bay.

#1 ENG HYD PUMP and #2 ENG HYD PUMP Caution Lights

One of these caution lights may be inoperative if its associated HYD PRESS Indication, ISOLATION VALVE, and hydraulic pressure are verified operative. Maintenance involves starting the engine and checking that the pressure gauge reads correctly and the "HYD ISO VLV" caution light extinguishes.

HYD QTY 1 and HYD QTY 2 Indications

One of these indications may be inoperative if the hydraulic fluid quantity is verified as adequate before each flight, and associated indicators are verified operative. The maintenance procedure includes checking fluid levels and then starting the engine to verify lights go out and pressure is normal.

#1 HYD FLUID HOT and #2 HYD FLUID HOT Caution Lights

One of these caution lights may be inoperative provided associated indications are operative and steps are taken to ensure an overheat condition has not occurred. Maintenance includes a detailed inspection for leaks and checking a fluid sample for discoloration 15-35 minutes after engine shutdown.

Ice Protection Systems

DEICE PRESS Caution Light

The DEICE PRESS caution light may be inoperative if all De-Ice Boot Advisory Lights and DEICE PRESS Indicators are verified operative. The maintenance procedure involves an operational test of the de-icing system, checking light sequences and pressure readings.

DEICE PRESS Indicators

The DEICE PRESS Indicators may be inoperative if all De-Ice Boot Advisory Lights and the DEICE PRESS Caution Light are verified operative. The maintenance procedure is similar to the above, but focuses on the caution light's operation during the test.

Low Pressure Warning Switches (LPWS)

A single Low Pressure Warning Switch may be inoperative if other de-ice system components are verified operative and the faulty switch is disconnected. Maintenance involves confirming other components work, disconnecting the faulty switch's electrical plug, and stowing it.

Engine Intake Bypass Door OPN and CLOSED Advisory Lights

These lights may be inoperative if the Bypass Door operation is verified prior to each flight and the related engine intake heater indicator is operative. Maintenance requires a visual check of the bypass doors' operation by pressing the switchlight.

ENGINE INTAKE HTR Advisory Lights

The ENGINE INTAKE HTR advisory lights may be inoperative if the associated heater and ENG ADPT HEAT Caution Light are operative. Maintenance involves verifying the heater by monitoring the AC generator load increase/decrease when the switch is pressed and verifying the caution lights operate normally.

ENG ADPT HEAT Caution Lights

The ENG ADPT HEAT caution lights may be inoperative if the associated heater and advisory light are operative. Maintenance involves verifying the heater and advisory light by monitoring the AC generator load and HTR light when the switch is pressed.

Engine Intake Adapter Heater Assemblies

An Engine Intake Adapter Heater Assembly may be inoperative if the flight is not conducted in known or forecast icing conditions. The maintenance procedure simply requires pulling and collaring the circuit breaker associated with the inoperative heater.

PITOT HEAT 1, PITOT HEAT 2 and PITOT HEAT STBY Caution Lights

These lights may be inoperative if the flight is not in known or forecast icing conditions and all other elements of the pitot heat systems are verified operative. Maintenance procedures require a physical check to confirm the pitot probes have warmed up when heat is applied.

Co-Pilot Heated Windshield

The Co-Pilot Heated Windshield may be inoperative for one flight day under specific conditions. The maintenance procedure involves interrogating the Centralized Diagnostic System (CDS) and, if necessary, performing an operational test on the Pilot's side systems.

Ice Detector Spigot Illumination Lights

These lights may be inoperative for night operations for one flight day if both Ice Detector Probes are operative, there are no known or forecast icing conditions, and an operable flashlight is available. The maintenance procedure requires interrogating the CDS to verify no "ICE DETECTOR FAIL" messages are displayed.

Flight Recorders and Avionics

Flight Data Recorder

The Flight Data Recorder can be inoperative if the Cockpit Voice Recorder is operative and repairs are made within three flight days. The maintenance procedure for this involves pulling and clipping the FDR circuit breaker and then placarding the inoperative recorder on the Overhead Console. An entry must also be made in the aircraft journey log.

EFIS/ESID Display Units

The RH MFD (MFD2) can be inoperative for one flight day provided specific conditions are met. Maintenance procedures may include pulling circuit breakers to re-arrange displays, physically swapping display units, and verifying functionality of the remaining displays. The inoperative MFD2 is then placarded, and a log entry is made.

Landing Gear and Brakes

Parking Brake Pressure Indications (HYD PRESS PK BRK Indication)

The HYD PRESS PK BRK Indication in the flight compartment may be inoperative if the Aft Wing Root Fairing Indicator is verified as operative and pressure is confirmed adequate before engine start. Maintenance involves cycling the park brake lever, ensuring the accumulator gas charge is sufficient, placarding the indicator, and making a log entry.

Parking Brake Pressure Indications (Aft Wing Root Fairing Indicator)

The Aft Wing Root Fairing Indicator may be inoperative if the flight compartment indicator is operative and pressure is confirmed adequate. The maintenance procedure is to ensure the MFD indicator shows at least 1500 psi, using a hand pump if necessary. The inoperative gauge must be placarded and a log entry made.

Nosewheel Steering

The Nosewheel Steering system may be inoperative for one flight day if deactivated. The maintenance procedure requires pulling and clipping the NLG STEER CONT circuit breakers, placarding the switch and caution light, and making a log entry.

Touched Runway Indication System

The Touched Runway Indication System may be inoperative if it's confirmed before each flight that the frangible switch has not been contacted. Maintenance involves a visual inspection for damage. If the warning light is stuck on, the LEDs must be removed. The warning light must be placarded, and a log entry made.

Lighting Systems

Caution/Warning/Advisory Lights System (Dimming Function)

The dimming function may be inoperative for day operations. The maintenance procedure involves performing a test of the lights to confirm illumination. The glareshield must then be placarded with "Caution/Warning Lights/Advisory Lights Dim Mode INOP".

Caution/Warning/Advisory Lights System (Brighten Function)

The brighten function may be inoperative for day operations if the dimming function is also considered inoperative. The DIM/BRT switch must not be operated and must be placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT USE". Maintenance includes a light test, and the glareshield must be placarded.

Cabin Lighting System (Sidewall Lighting)

Up to 25% of the total length of sidewall downwash lights may be inoperative under certain conditions. Inoperative lights must be placarded at the flight attendant's LIGHTING panel.

Cabin Lighting System (Ceiling Lighting)

Up to 25% of the total length of ceiling upwash lights may be inoperative under certain conditions. Inoperative lights must be placarded at the flight attendant's LIGHTING panel.

Lavatory Compartment (RETURN TO SEAT Lights/Signs)

The lavatory's RETURN TO SEAT lights can be inoperative if the lavatory door is locked closed and placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT ENTER". Maintenance involves placarding the light and door, and physically locking the door.

Aft Service Light

The Aft Service Light may be inoperative if deactivated. The maintenance procedure is to open and clip the "GND SERVICE LIGHT" circuit breaker.

A/COL WHITE Lights

One bulb at each position may be inoperative, provided the remaining operative bulbs are selected for use. Maintenance involves accessing the Anti-Collision Light Selector Panel and toggling the switch to the operative bulb. The inoperative lights must be placarded.

Navigation and Warning Systems

Traffic Alert and Collision Avoidance System (TCAS)

The TCAS can be inoperative for up to 36 hours if it is deactivated and secured. Maintenance requires pulling and clipping the TCAS circuit breaker, placarding the Glareshield, and making a log entry.

GPWS I/F FAIL Engine Display Message

A GPWS I/F FAIL message may be displayed provided the GPWS is considered inoperative. The maintenance procedure is to pull and clip the GPWS circuit breaker (A1), placard the glareshield with "GPWS INOP," and make a log entry.

Radio Altimeters

Two radio altimeters may be inoperative for up to two flight days provided the GPWS/EGPWS and TCAS are also considered inoperative. The procedure requires pulling and clipping the circuit breakers for RAD ALT 1, RAD ALT 2, STICK PUSHER, GPWS/EGPWS, and TCAS, and placarding the systems as inoperative. A single radio altimeter may be inoperative by pulling its corresponding circuit breaker and placarding the glareshield. A log entry is required in all cases.

Oxygen and Bleed Air Systems

Flight Deck Fixed Oxygen System OXYGEN Pressure Gauges

One of the two oxygen pressure gauges may be inoperative if there is another way to confirm adequate oxygen pressure. Maintenance involves placarding the inoperative gauge and checking the pressure using the operative gauge before each flight.

First Aid Oxygen System

One of the two First Aid Oxygen System bottles may be unserviceable or missing, provided the required number of operative units are maintained. Maintenance involves removing or securing inoperative bottles and placarding their locations.

BLEED SYSTEMS

One bleed system may be inoperative if the associated BLEED Switch is selected OFF and the flight is not in icing conditions. If the BLEED HOT Caution Light stays on, maintenance must interrogate the CDS. Depending on the fault, a switch may need repair/replacement or can be disconnected by jumpering pins on its electrical connector. The inoperative system must be placarded.

Water and Waste Systems

Warm Water Wash System

The Warm Water Wash System may be inoperative if it is drained, inspected for leaks, and placarded. Components must be deactivated or isolated. The system must be placarded and a log entry made.

Drain Mast Heaters

A Drain Mast Heater may be inoperative if flights are not in freezing conditions. The maintenance procedure includes verifying it doesn't affect other heaters, potentially opening and collaring the "DRAIN MAST" circuit breaker, and placarding the system.

Lavatory Waste System

Individual components may be inoperative if they are deactivated or isolated and inspected for leaks. If the entire system is inoperative, the waste tank must be drained, the LAVATORY FLUSH circuit breaker opened and clipped, and the lavatory door locked and placarded "INOPERATIVE - DO NOT ENTER".

Other Systems

Centralized Diagnostic System (CDS)

The CDS may be inoperative. The maintenance procedure is to placard the Central Maintenance Panel with "CDS INOP" and make a log entry.

Propeller Maintenance Unfeather Function

This function may be inoperative if an alternate feather check is performed. The procedure involves visually confirming the propeller blades move to the feathered position when ALT FEATHER is selected. The affected switch is then placarded as "INOP".

PEC System: PEC faults

If a PEC fault occurs, the propeller can be dispatched with the fault, but it must be repaired within a specific time limit. The maintenance procedure requires placarding the inoperative PEC System in the flight compartment and making a log entry.

Engine Monitoring Unit (EMU)

The EMU can be inoperative if alternative procedures are used to record engine data. The procedure involves placarding the inoperative EMU and notifying a monitoring agency that data will be recorded manually.

Engine FADEC System: FADEC faults

The aircraft can be dispatched with FADEC faults if repairs are made within the time limits specified for Time Limited Dispatch (TLD). Certain fault codes will cause the POWERPLANT caption to appear, meaning no further flights are allowed. The procedure is to placard the inoperative system and make a log entry.

Door Seal System Charging Valve

This valve may be inoperative if it is secured in the closed position. Maintenance involves accessing the valve and installing a steel cap. The inoperative valve must be placarded and a log entry made.

Door Seal Drain Valve

This valve may be failed in the open position if the door seal can still be pressurized. Maintenance involves removing the drain line and installing plugs and caps on the reservoir tank and valve. The valve is then placarded.

"INTERNAL BAGG DOOR" Caution Light

This light may be inoperative if the internal baggage door is secured closed and placarded "INTERNAL BAGGAGE DOOR INOPERATIVE - DO NOT OPEN". Maintenance involves securing the door and placarding it. If the light is illuminated, the LEDs are removed to extinguish it.

Internal Baggage Door Handle

If the handle cannot be latched or unlatched, the door can be considered inoperative if secured closed and placarded. Maintenance involves entering the baggage compartment via the external door to confirm and secure the internal door.

FUSELAGE DOORS Warning System (Proximity Sensors)

One proximity sensor may be inoperative for one flight day. The maintenance procedure requires using the ESID to identify the faulty sensor, deactivating it, physically verifying the door is locked, and checking that the warning light re-triggers with another door. The sensor is then placarded.

Electro Pneumatic Shut-Off Valve (EPSOV)

The EPSOV may be inoperative if it is verified open and the Cabin Pressure Control System's AUTO Mode is operative. Maintenance involves pulling and clipping the AIRSTAIR DOOR SOV circuit breaker and verifying the door seal inflates and deflates correctly.

Aft Baggage Door Counter Balance System & Telescopic Strut

These systems may be inoperative if the door is verified closed, locked, and placarded "BAGGAGE DOOR INOPERATIVE - DO NOT OPEN".

Fortified Flight Deck Door (With Remote Access System)

If certain functions are inoperative, the system must be deactivated by pulling and clipping the CKPT DOOR circuit breaker, and the secondary deadbolt must be used. The system is then placarded. For inoperative advisory lights, maintenance involves verifying the operation of all other door warning systems and placarding the inoperative light.

APU Bleed Air System

The APU Bleed Air System may be inoperative if the APU Bleed Valve is verified closed. Maintenance is a visual confirmation of the valve's position. The APU BL AIR button is then placarded.

Auxiliary Power Unit (APU)

The APU may be inoperative if the APU Airframe Fuel Shutoff Valve is verified closed. Verification can be done visually with a borescope/access panel or functionally by pulling a circuit breaker and attempting a start. The inoperative APU must be placarded on the control panel.
