Propeller System Overview

General Characteristics

How does the propeller convert engine power into thrust?

The propeller assembly converts engine-supplied power into usable thrust for aircraft propulsion during flight and ground maneuvers.

What is the model number of the propeller, and what do its components stand for?

The model number is CR408/6-123-F/17. The breakdown is:

  • C: Civil
  • R: Dowty Aerospace Propellers
  • 408: Aircraft type identification
  • 6: Number of blades
  • 123: Blade root end size in mm
  • F: Flange mounted
  • 17: Function / Installation characteristics

What is the propeller's diameter?

The diameter is 13.5 ft (4.11 m).

Components and Materials

What are the main components of the propeller assembly?

The main components are the Propeller Blade Assembly, Hub Assembly, and Spinner Assembly.

What are the major components of the propeller?

  • Blades
  • Hub
  • Crosshead
  • Beta Tube Assembly
  • Pitch Change Cylinder and Piston
  • Blade Bearings
  • Blade Counterweights

What materials are the propeller blades made of?

Each blade has an all-composite airfoil construction with a steel outer root sleeve. The airfoil has a foam core and twin carbon fiber spars with an overall braided carbon/glass fiber shell.

What is the purpose of the spinner?

The spinner provides an aerodynamic fairing over the front end of the propeller.

Installation and Operation

How is the propeller installed on the engine reduction gearbox?

The propeller is installed on the engine reduction gearbox propeller drive flange using 15 threaded studs and nuts. Three location dowels also assist with correct installation and transmit some of the drive torque.

How is torque transmitted from the engine reduction gearbox to the propeller?

Torque is transmitted from the reduction gearbox through three dowels and the friction of the flange interface. The dowels account for approximately 50% of the torque transmission, and the flange friction accounts for the other 50%.

How is electrical power transferred for blade deicing?

A slipring is used to transfer electrical power for blade deicing. The slipring is installed directly onto the backplate of the hub assembly and has bronze rings in a plastic molding.

Control Systems

What is the Propeller Electronic Control (PEC), and what does it do?

The Propeller Electronic Control (PEC) is a dual-channel, microprocessor-based controller that uses inputs from the aircraft, propeller control system sensors, and the engine control system to control propeller pitch and speed.

What components are electronically connected to the PEC?

The PEC is electronically connected to the condition lever, power lever (through FADEC), Magnetic Pick-up Unit (MPU), Beta Feedback Transducer (BFT), Pitch Control Unit (PCU), and the Full Authority Digital Electronic Control (FADEC).

What is the function of the Pitch Control Unit (PCU)?

The Pitch Control Unit (PCU) is a hydromechanical device commanded by the PEC. It meters high-pressure engine oil to a servo valve and controls the flow of oil to the fine or coarse pitch chambers of the propeller to move the blades in the desired direction.

Pitch Control

How is the propeller's blade pitch changed?

Blade pitch is changed using an electronically controlled hydraulic pitch actuation system, which is accomplished by metered oil pressure.

What is the Beta Mode?

The Beta range is from a power lever position above FLIGHT IDLE to the full reverse (MAX REV) position. When the propeller is in Beta mode, the blade angle is set by the power lever input.

What is the function of the 'hard' and 'soft' flight fine stops?

The hydraulic 'hard' flight fine stop limits the fine pitch in the constant speeding mode to approximately 16°. The function of this stop is to maintain a minimum pitch consistent with a positive counterweight effort towards coarse pitch, ensuring the effectiveness of the OSG. A 'soft' stop of approximately 16.5 degrees is programmed into the PEC and is operative while the Power Lever is at or above Flight Idle.

How is the propeller speed controlled in Reverse Speed Control mode?

In this mode, the system operates in a closed-loop propeller RPM control, maintaining the propeller speed between 660 and 950 rpm. The engine schedules fuel based on a power schedule versus the Power Lever Angle (PLA).

Safety and Protection Systems

What happens in the event of an oil pressure failure?

The propeller blades are counter-weighted to move towards coarse pitch in the event of an oil pressure failure.

What is the purpose of the Propeller Overspeed Governor Unit (OSG)?

The Propeller Overspeed Governor Unit is an independent mechanical system that protects the propeller from overspeeding.

What happens during an overspeed condition?

Propeller RPM is reduced by increasing blade angles.

What happens if the OSG fails to control an overspeed condition?

If the OSG fails, the FADEC will reduce engine fuel at approximately 1122 Np, which will cause the Np to decrease.

How is the propeller tested on the ground?

The OSG can be tested on the ground by operating the PROP O'SPEED GOVERNOR test switch on the Pilots Side Panel on the flight deck.

What is the function of the Automatic Underspeed Protection Circuit (AUPC)?

The AUPC is a circuit in the PEC designed to prevent a catastrophic event from an unsolicited coarse pitch demand due to a failure. It sends a "drive fine" signal to the PCU during propeller underspeeding to ensure total aircraft thrust loss does not fall below the thrust of a single engine.

Feathering Systems

What are the three propeller feathering systems?

The propeller feathering systems provide autofeathering, alternate feathering and unfeathering, and manual feathering.

What is the purpose of the Propeller Autofeather system?

The autofeather system provides automatically initiated propeller feathering and a power uptrim to the operating engine following an engine failure during takeoff.

When is the autofeather system armed?

The ARM light turns on when both engine torques exceed 50%, and both power levers are advanced beyond 60° PLA.

When is autofeather triggered from the armed state?

It is triggered when the local engine's torque falls below 25% for at least three seconds.

What happens when one propeller is autofeathered?

The autofeather function of the other propeller is automatically disarmed.

How can the autofeather system be disarmed?

It can be disarmed by pushing the AUTOFEATHER switchlight OFF, retarding one or both power levers to flight idle, or when both engine torques drop below approximately 50%.

What is the function of the Auxiliary Feathering Pump Unit?

The Auxiliary Feathering Pump Unit provides an independent way to feather the propeller if the primary feathering system fails. It is also used to unfeather the propeller on the ground for maintenance.

How is the auxiliary feathering pump activated during an autofeather event?

The pump is activated for approximately 30 seconds to ensure adequate oil pressure for feathering.

What is the procedure for alternate feathering?

Alternate feathering is done by operating the #1 or #2 ALT FTHR switch on the PROPELLER control panel with the Condition Lever at Start/Feather or Fuel Off.

Additional Systems

What is the purpose of the Propeller Synchrophasing System?

The Propeller Synchrophasing System reduces cabin noise by controlling the relative phase difference between the slave and master propellers to a demanded angle.

How is the phase angle calculated by this system?

The phase angle is calculated by timing the differences between the master and slave propeller Magnetic Pickup Unit (MPU) signals over a complete propeller revolution.
