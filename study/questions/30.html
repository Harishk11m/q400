Aircraft System Q&A

What happens when the IDS senses ice accumulation?

  • The words "ICE DETECTED" appear on the Engine Display (ED).

What controls does the IDS have in the flight compartment?

  • The IDS has no flight compartment controls and operates automatically when 115 V ac power is available.

What components interface with the Electronic Instrument System (EIS) to generate the "ICE DETECTED" message?

  • The Integrated Flight Cabinets (IFCs) interface with the EIS to generate the advisory message.

On which principle does the Ice Detector Probe (IDP) work?

  • The ice sensor in the IDP operates on a piezoelectric principle.

What happens to the frequency of the ice sensor when ice accumulates on it?

  • When ice accumulates on the sensor, the frequency of vibration increases due to an increase in rigidity.

What happens to the natural frequency of the sensor when water or liquid contaminants accumulate?

  • Water or liquid contaminants increase the diaphragm mass without increasing its stiffness, causing the natural frequency to drop.

What is the function of the signal conditioner?

  • The signal conditioner reads the signal from the ice sensor and compares its frequency to a stored "Ice Detection Threshold Level Frequency".

If the REF SPEEDS switch is selected to OFF, what message will appear on the ED when ice is detected?

  • An "ICE DETECTED" message will flash in amber (yellow) in normal video below the SAT indication.

What happens if the REF SPEEDS switch is selected to INCR after ice is initially detected?

  • The "ICE DETECTED" message will change to reverse white video for 5 seconds.

What happens if the REF SPEEDS switch is selected to INCR and an IDP detects ice after 5 seconds?

  • The "ICE DETECTED" message will display as steady white.

What message appears when the REF SPEEDS switch is in the INCR position?

  • An "INCR REF SPEED" message will display in white below the "ICE DETECTED" message, confirming that the Stall Protection System (SPS) is modified for icing conditions.

What happens if both IDPs fail?

  • The "ICE DETECT FAIL" caution light will come on.

What happens if only one IDP fails?

  • The caution light will not come on because the system is redundant.

For how long will the "ICE DETECTED" message remain in view?

  • The message will remain in view for a minimum of 60 seconds.

What happens when one IDP fails?

  • The failure is reported to the TMU, which stores it as a maintenance message.
  • This message is then displayed on the ESID as "LEFT IDP FAIL" or "RIGHT IDP FAIL" when the Centralized Diagnostic System (CDS) is interrogated.

What is the regulated pressure at which the system operates?

  • The system operates at a regulated pressure of 18 psig (124 kPag).

When does the Isolation Shut-off Valve (ISOV) normally open?

  • The ISOV normally opens as soon as 28 Vdc electrical power supply is available, ensuring that deicing air is available to both sides of the aircraft in case of a failure on one side.

How long do the deice cycles last in SLOW and FAST mode?

  • The deice cycle lasts 3 minutes in SLOW mode and 1 minute in FAST mode.

How many sequences are in a cycle?

  • The cycle is divided into 6 sequences of inflation and a dwell period.
  • Each sequence consists of inflation of an individual boot for 6 seconds, followed by suck down of that boot.

How is air supplied to the boots?

  • Air is supplied from the engines through a Pressure Regulating Valve (PRV) with a Check Valve (CV) located in the nacelle.

What happens if one of the supply branches to the tail ruptures?

  • Check valves prevent the complete loss of pneumatic supply to the tail boots.

What is the function of the Timer and Monitor Unit (TMU) in relation to the DDVs?

  • The TMU compares the command signal and the Low Pressure Switch (LPS) signal to determine if the DDV operation is normal.
  • If the TMU commands an output to open and the related LPS does not send a signal back, it indicates a fault, such as the DDV not opening or a burst boot.

What happens if the TMU receives a signal from an LPS that has not been commanded?

  • This indicates that the DDV operation is not normal.

What happens when the TMU senses a DDV fault?

  • The TMU sends a discrete signal to the Caution and Warning panel, and the DEICE PRESS caution light comes on.

What happens if one Pitot/Static probe or Angle of Attack (AOA) vane heater has no current flow?

  • The TMU records a failure signal and sends it to the avionics system (Stall Protection Modules 1 and 2).

What is the function of the Non-Heated Check Valves (NHCVs)?

  • The NHCVs prevent moisture from flowing back into the wing system in the event of a failure.
  • A faulty NHCV would not prevent the de-icing system from functioning.

What is the purpose of the Low-Pressure Switch (LPS) in each DDV?

  • The LPS closes when the pressure rises above 15 ± 1 psig, sending a signal to the TMU to confirm that the commanded valve has opened and the pressure was sufficient to inflate the boots.
  • The LPS opens when the pressure drops below 11 +2.5/-1 psig, indicating that the valve has operated normally.

What is the normal state of the Isolation Shut-off Valve (ISOV)?

  • The ISOV is normally closed when not energized and opens automatically when an engine is started.
  • It can be closed by the flight crew to isolate the two sides in the event of a pressure loss.

How does the Dual Distributing Valve (DDV) allow the boots to flatten?

  • At boot deflation, the electrical valve allows air to flow back to the DDV and exhaust the system through the ejector.
  • Air is removed from the boots using a venturi effect, which allows them to flatten on the leading edge.

What prevents the complete loss of pneumatic supply to the tail boots if a supply branch ruptures?

  • Check valves prevent the complete loss of pneumatic supply to the tail boots if one of the supply branches ruptures.

What causes the PITOT HEAT 1, PITOT HEAT 2, or PITOT HEAT STBY caution light to come on?

  • These caution lights come on when there is no current flow into the heating element of one Pitot/Static probe or when the timer is not powered.

What happens when a failure of an Angle of Attack (AOA) vane heater is detected?

  • A failure signal is recorded in the Timer and Monitor Unit (TMU) and is sent to the avionics system (Stall Protection Modules 1 and 2).

What would a failure of both PRRVs lead to?

  • A failure of both PRRVs would lead to a total loss of the pneumatic supply and, therefore, the total loss of airframe de-icing.

What is the effect of a PRRV failure?

  • A failure of one PRRV does not prevent the system from operating normally.
  • However, a failure of both PRRVs would lead to a total loss of the pneumatic supply and, therefore, the total loss of airframe de-icing.

What are the components of the engine intake anti-icing system?

  • The components are Thermostats, Air Intake Heater; Switches, Heater-Engine Intake; and Relays, Control-Intake Heater.

What conditions must be met for power to be supplied to the heater?

  • Power is supplied to the heater when all of the following conditions are met: the temperature measured by a thermostat is below +60 °F (+15.6 °C), the related engine oil pressure switch is closed, the ENGINE INTAKE switch light has been pushed, and 115 Vac power is available.

What happens if both heating elements in the engine adapter heater fail?

  • The related ENG ADPT HEAT 1 or ENG ADPT HEAT 2 caution light on the Caution and Warning panel comes on.

What other de-ice boots operate at the same time as the left nacelle deicer boot?

  • The left nacelle deicer boot operates at the same time as the inboard horizontal stabilizer deicer boots.

What other de-ice boots operate at the same time as the right nacelle deicer boot?

  • The right nacelle deicer boot operates at the same time as the outboard horizontal stabilizer deicer boots.
